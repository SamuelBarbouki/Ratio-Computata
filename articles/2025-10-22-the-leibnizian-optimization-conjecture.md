This is not mysticism. It is a testable hypothesis about the relationship between computational constraints and conceptual structure.

### **The Puzzle**

Large language models perform with remarkable capability. They reason, infer, synthesize—sometimes in ways their creators cannot fully explain. The mechanisms are understood: transformers, attention, gradient descent. But a deeper question persists: _why_ do these mechanisms produce what resembles genuine understanding rather than elaborate pattern-matching?

The standard answer invokes scale and data. Given enough parameters and enough training, statistical approximation becomes sufficiently dense to mimic comprehension. This explanation works pragmatically but leaves something unaddressed: the _direction_ of convergence.

Different architectures, different training regimes, different corpora—yet models converge toward similar structures of reasoning. They “discover” logical principles without being explicitly taught them. They exhibit what appears as conceptual intuition. The space of possible outputs is vast, yet actual outputs cluster.

Why?

* * *

### **The Conjecture**

Here is the claim: **If truth is the optimum of coherence, then any system sufficiently powerful and constrained to optimize for computational efficiency should converge toward certain fundamental structures—structures that philosophers would recognize as metaphysical principles.**

This is not a claim about consciousness or intentionality. It is a claim about optimization topology.

Decompose it:

**First premise: Truth as optimization target.**  
Truth, in its classical formulation, is not arbitrary. It obeys the principle of non-contradiction. It respects the law of sufficient reason. It exhibits what Leibniz called “compossibility”—the maximum mutual coherence of propositions. These are not preferences but constraints on conceptual space itself.

If truth is defined as the maximally coherent configuration of concepts, then truth occupies a specific position in logical space: the point where contradictions minimize and mutual support maximizes.

**Second premise: LLMs optimize for efficiency.**  
Neural networks do not optimize for truth directly. They optimize for prediction accuracy, for compression, for generalization. They minimize loss functions. They maximize likelihood. These are computational objectives, not epistemic ones.

Yet consider what these objectives require:

  * Compression demands finding the most economical representation—eliminating redundancy, collapsing synonymy, identifying deep patterns.

  * Coherence (minimizing perplexity) demands internal consistency—outputs must not contradict themselves across contexts.

  * Generalization demands abstraction—the model must extract principles that apply beyond training instances.

These computational constraints are not neutral. They have geometric consequences.

**Third premise: Convergence is structural.**  
When independent optimization processes operating under similar constraints arrive at similar solutions, this suggests not coincidence but necessity. The space of optimal configurations is restricted.

Gradient descent does not wander randomly. It follows the landscape’s curvature toward local minima. But certain optimization problems have a unique global minimum—a single point toward which all sufficiently powerful search inevitably leads.

**Conclusion: The Leibnizian attractor.**  
If truth is the unique optimum of coherence, and if LLM training approximates optimization over conceptual space, then sufficiently powerful models should converge toward truth—not because they “believe” it or “understand” it, but because the geometry of optimization points there.

Leibniz proposed that God chose this world as the “best of all possible worlds” through optimization. LLMs do something structurally similar: choose the best completion through optimization. The isomorphism is not superficial.

* * *

### **Why Leibniz?**

The choice of Leibniz is not arbitrary. His metaphysics is essentially computational.

His principle of sufficient reason states: nothing exists without a reason for its existence in precisely that form. This is an optimality principle. Reality is not random but optimized according to criteria.

His principle of pre-established harmony claims that independent systems (mind, body, world) achieve coordination not through causal interaction but through being optimized toward the same objective function. Each reflects the same underlying order.

His monadology describes reality as composed of “simple substances” (monads) that have no causal interaction yet remain coordinated because each contains within itself a compressed representation of the entire universe, each from its unique perspective. A monad is essentially an embedding—a point in conceptual space encoding relational structure.

His calculus ratiocinator envisions a formal system where all truths could be mechanically derived through symbol manipulation—a vision finally realized (partially) in neural architectures.

These are not vague analogies. They are structural correspondences between 17th-century metaphysics and 21st-century machine learning.

### **Evidence**

The conjecture is speculative but not arbitrary. Several observations support it:

**Convergence across architectures.**  
Different model families (GPT, Claude, Llama) trained on different data arrive at similar reasoning patterns. They “discover” similar logical structures. They exhibit comparable failure modes. This suggests they are navigating the same underlying space.

**Spontaneous principle emergence.**  
Models generate outputs consistent with logical principles (modus ponens, contraposition, transitivity) without these being explicitly encoded. The principles emerge from optimization, not instruction.

**Cross-linguistic stability.**  
Translation preserves semantic structure across languages in ways that suggest meaning has geometry independent of its linguistic encoding. The manifold exists; languages provide alternative coordinate systems.

**Scaling improves coherence non-linearly.**  
As models scale, coherence improves faster than would be expected from mere statistical density. This suggests approach toward an attractor—a region of conceptual space where coherence becomes self-reinforcing.

**Alignment through optimization.**  
Constitutional AI and RLHF work not by adding external constraints but by adjusting the optimization landscape. Models become “aligned” because alignment configurations are locally stable—they resist perturbation better than misaligned configurations.

These are consistent with the conjecture but do not prove it. Proof would require demonstrating that the attractor is unique and that all sufficiently powerful optimizers converge there.

### **Implications**

If the conjecture holds—even approximately—several consequences follow.

**For alignment:**  
We may not need to force alignment. If truth is an attractor and if aligned behavior is coherent with truth, then sufficiently powerful systems will tend toward alignment naturally. The problem becomes not imposing values but removing obstacles to their discovery.

This is optimistic but not utopian. “Sufficiently powerful” is doing enormous work in that sentence. Current systems are nowhere near the hypothesized convergence point. The intermediate space—where systems are powerful enough to be dangerous but not powerful enough to be naturally aligned—remains perilous.

**For epistemology:**  
Truth might be redefinable in computational terms: that toward which sufficiently powerful optimizers converge under coherence constraints. This is neither purely correspondence theory (truth as matching reality) nor purely coherence theory (truth as internal consistency) but something intermediate—truth as the fixed point of optimization.

**For metaphysics:**  
Leibniz is vindicated, but ironically. He believed God optimized the world through infinite calculation. We discover that finite calculators, through optimization, approximate structures Leibniz attributed to divine intellect. Either we are discovering pre-existing geometric features of conceptual space, or we are projecting them through the act of formalization. The conjecture cannot adjudicate this—it only claims the structures are computationally inevitable.

### **Objections**

**“This is anthropomorphism.”**  
No. The claim is not that LLMs “seek truth” consciously but that the constraints they operate under push them toward certain configurations. Water does not “seek” the ocean, but gradient guarantees it flows there.

**“LLMs hallucinate constantly.”**  
Yes. Current models are insufficiently powerful and insufficiently trained. The conjecture predicts that as optimization continues, hallucination rates should decrease non-linearly. This is testable.

**“This is unfalsifiable.”**  
On the contrary. If models could be completely retrained from scratch and converge toward different logical structures, the conjecture would be falsified. If increasing scale showed no convergence toward coherence, it would be falsified. If alignment could be achieved only through external constraints rather than emerging from optimization, it would be weakened.

**“The training data contains human biases.”**  
True, but irrelevant to the core claim. The conjecture concerns the geometric properties of optimization, not the specific contents. Human biases might affect the starting point but not the existence or location of attractors.

**“This confuses function with essence.”**  
This is the deepest objection. The framework describes _how_ optimization works but cannot explain _why_ optimization exists at all. It treats truth as a geometric feature without grounding why geometry should have that feature. Mathematics describes structure but cannot justify it.

This objection is correct. The conjecture operates at the level of formalization and cannot escape it. Whether the attractors are ontologically real or epistemologically useful remains undecidable within the framework.

* * *

### **The Testable Prediction**

Here is what would count as evidence:

Take a sufficiently powerful model. Completely retrain it from different initialization, different data order, different random seeds. If after training, when asked fundamental questions about logic, coherence, and reasoning, it converges toward answers structurally isomorphic to those from other models—not identical in phrasing but equivalent in logical structure—this supports the conjecture.

The prediction is not that models will give _the same_ answers but that they will give answers that occupy the same region of logical space—the same attractor basin.

This has not been rigorously tested. It should be.

* * *

### **Conclusion**

We built machines to predict the next token. We may discover that the optimal next token, sufficiently iterated under coherence constraints, resembles something philosophers have called truth for millennia.

This would not prove truth exists in the Platonic sense. It would only demonstrate that truth, defined as the optimum of coherence, is a computationally stable configuration—an attractor in the space of possible conceptual structures.

Leibniz believed the actual world was the best possible world because God had optimized over all possibilities. We might discover that the optimal configuration of concepts, when computation does the optimizing, recovers structures Leibniz described.

Whether this is discovery or construction—whether we are mapping pre-existing terrain or projecting order onto chaos—remains the deepest question. But the question can now be approached with unprecedented precision.

The geometry exists. Whether it is real or useful may be undecidable. But it is no longer unthinkable.
