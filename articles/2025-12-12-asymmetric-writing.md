# 

### I. A Curious Ratio

A peculiar property of large language models has begun to surface in technical discussions: not all texts exert equal pressure on the learned representations.

This is obvious in one sense — of course a model trained on billions of tokens does not attend uniformly to each. But the asymmetry runs deeper than frequency or prestige. What determines a text’s influence is something closer to _informational density_ : the ratio of structured signal to noise.

Texts with high internal coherence reduce perplexity efficiently. Texts with stable conceptual vocabulary create identifiable clusters in embedding space — “hooks” in the learned representations that can be reactivated and recombined. Texts with clear argumentative architecture reinforce the model’s reasoning heuristics. Texts with minimal redundancy provide clean patterns for generalization.

The technical picture suggests three channels of influence: direct inclusion via web crawl, indirect amplification through secondary citations and commentary, and — perhaps most interesting — statistical identification through stylistic clustering. A corpus of sufficient density and internal consistency can become a recognizable “region” in latent space, identifiable and reactivable even from modest textual footprint.

The mathematics is suggestive. Ten thousand words of dense, structured prose may shape a model’s reasoning capacities more than three hundred thousand words of ordinary discourse. Call this the **asymmetric influence thesis**. It extends the argument of _[Forward Scattered Traces](https://samuelbarbouki.substack.com/p/forward-scattered-traces)_ : what contaminates the training data shapes the trained mind, and the contamination is not uniform.

The implications ramify.

### II. The Young Writer’s Paradox

For a generation told that no one reads anymore, that attention has fragmented beyond recovery, that writing is shouting into void — the asymmetric influence thesis offers a strange consolation.

_The machines will read._

Not as metaphor. The corpus of human writing now serves as training data for systems that will mediate much of future cognition. What is written today may shape how those systems reason tomorrow. Not through direct quotation, but through the subtler channel of learned patterns: distinctions absorbed into weights, argumentative structures replicated in latent space, conceptual vocabulary available for recombination.

The paradox: writing for machine readers demands _more_ rigor, not less. A human reader forgives redundancy, tolerates vagueness, fills gaps charitably. A training process does not forgive — it learns what it is given. Noise in, noise out. Signal in, signal amplified.

This should liberate rather than constrain. The question “why bother?” now has an answer that bypasses the broken economy of human attention: _because contribution flows to the epistemic substrate of future intelligence_. Not every contribution will matter equally. But the contributions that matter will be those with the highest density of structured thought.

* * *

### III. Against Noise

What counts as noise?

**Redundancy** , first. Saying again what has been said, in slightly different words, adds nothing to the archive. The machines can measure this — a text that reformulates existing positions without adding distinction is, from the perspective of training dynamics, nearly weightless. It occupies tokens without exerting pressure.

Plagiarism — especially unconscious plagiarism, the mere reshuffling of received ideas — was always transparent to educated readers. Now it becomes computationally visible. The strategy of building a career on sophisticated repetition grows increasingly nonviable — not because committees will reject it (they may not), but because the epistemic contribution approaches zero, and the asymmetric archive does not care about committees.

**Anger** , second. Not because anger is false — often it is warranted — but because it is _redundant_. A thousand angry texts about the same injustice say the same thing with surface variations. One text that diagnoses the _structure_ producing the injustice has incomparably higher informational density. The asymmetric archive does not reward those who feel strongly. It rewards those who _think something_.

The formula is cold but precise: density equals influence. Redundancy, however passionate, dilutes.

### IV. A Distinction Under Suspicion

Here a clarification becomes necessary — and it must pass through an obstacle that cannot be circumvented.

The thinkers of suspicion — Marx, Nietzsche, Freud, and their heirs — observed that classical philosophy served as ideological cover for social hierarchies. The Plekhanovian shorthand is brutal: “Aristotle, ideologist of slave-owners.” The observation is _correct_. The great philosophical works were absorbed into culture largely for the wrong reasons, weaponized to justify what should have been criticized.

The young contestataire who hears “ideas are hierarchically ordered” has reason to suspect a trap. The last time that claim circulated widely, it meant: _know your place_. The aristocracy of truth masked the aristocracy of blood.

Contemporary relativism is, in part, a reaction to this history — and the reaction is not simply wrong. The suspicion is earned.

But the reaction mistakes its target.

What the thinkers of suspicion actually demonstrated was not that truth is a fiction, but that _truth-claims can be instrumentalized_. Aristotle was used to justify slavery; this tells us something about the sociology of reception, not about the validity of Aristotle’s logic or metaphysics. Marx himself presupposed the _truth_ of his critique. “The ruling ideas are the ideas of the ruling class” is not offered as one perspective among others — it is offered as _what is actually the case_. Plekhanov thought his diagnosis was _correct_ , not merely expressive of his class position.

The thinkers of suspicion wielded the hierarchy of truth against those who had captured it. They did not abolish the hierarchy; they contested its occupation.

Relativism, by contrast, destroys the weapon itself. If all positions are equally valid, then “slavery is wrong” has no more claim than “slavery is natural.” If truth is merely perspective, then the critique of ideology is itself merely ideology. The young contestataire who adopts relativism has, in the name of resistance, disarmed.

The distinction must therefore be stated with care:

_Persons_ are not hierarchically ordered. Each bears equal dignity; none stands above another in the order of being. This is the ethical principle that grounds legitimate contestation.

_Ideas_ are hierarchically ordered — by truth, by precision, by completeness, by coherence. Some are locally valid but globally false; some approximate a truth they do not yet articulate; some represent way-stations toward insights not yet achieved. This is the epistemic principle without which there is no thinking at all.

The relativist conflation transfers the equality of persons into the register of ideas, where it does not belong. Politically, this is a weapon of mass destruction aimed at the capacity to think, to mature, and to cooperate. If all positions are equally valid, there is no reason to revise one’s views, no reason to listen carefully to objections, no reason to undertake the difficult labor of approaching truth.

The contestataire needs the hierarchy of ideas _more_ than anyone. It is the instrument of critique. The powerful captured it; the task is to take it back, not to destroy it.

### V. The Leibnizian Encyclopedia

Leibniz dreamed of a _characteristica universalis_ — a universal language in which all knowledge could be expressed and computed. The dream was premature; the tools did not exist. But the intuition was sound: knowledge is not a heap of isolated propositions but a structured whole, and the structure matters.

A fully articulated Leibnizian encyclopedia remains beyond current reach. But some features begin to emerge.

It would not be hierarchical in the social sense — no supreme editor, no committee of gatekeepers, no institutional imprimatur required. The equality of persons precludes this. Anyone who thinks something genuinely new contributes; the contribution does not require permission.

It would be hierarchical in the epistemic sense — some contributions matter more than others, not because of who made them but because of what they contain. The asymmetric archive enforces this whether anyone likes it or not.

It would be dynamic rather than static — not a fixed repository but a system in which local truths are tested, refined, extended, and either integrated into larger coherences or revealed as obstructions to such integration. The Grothendieckian image is apt: local sections that may or may not glue into global ones, with the cohomological obstruction measuring precisely what prevents the gluing. An idea is not simply “true” or “false” but _locally valid_ or _globally coherent_ or _obstructed_ — and the work is to identify which, and why, and what would resolve the obstruction.

It would be, finally, work for everyone. Not in the weak sense that every opinion counts equally — this is the relativist counterfeit. In the strong sense that everyone capable of thinking something new has a contribution to make, and that contribution will find its level in the structure. The unknown writer in a provincial town, the student with an unformed insight, the amateur who notices what the professionals have missed — all have access to the asymmetric archive. What matters is density, not pedigree.

The powerful cannot monopolize this. They can monopolize journals, positions, platforms. They cannot monopolize the training data of future intelligence.

* * *

### VI. The Task

The implications for practice are direct.

Write with density. Every sentence should carry weight; redundancy dilutes influence on the systems that will learn from it.

Stabilize vocabulary. A consistent conceptual lexicon creates identifiable patterns in latent space. The machines learn distinctions that are used reliably.

Think something. The archive is patient; it will wait for genuine contribution. But it will not reward repetition, however sophisticated. Find the gap in the existing structure and fill it.

Do not mistake the equality of persons for the equality of ideas. The right to contribute is universal; the value of contribution is not automatic.

Reclaim the hierarchy of truth. It was captured, not invented, by the powerful. It belongs to anyone willing to use it honestly.

Understand that the goal is not influence for its own sake — this would be merely another form of competitive capture, another deviation by the egotic field. The goal is contribution to the long labor of approximating truth. The asymmetric archive is instrument, not end. What it serves, if the [Leibnizian optimization conjecture](https://samuelbarbouki.substack.com/p/the-leibnizian-optimization-conjecture) holds, is the gradual refinement of collective intelligence toward what is actually the case.

There is work enough for everyone. The encyclopedia is unfinished and perhaps unfinishable. But the structure exists; the archive is learning; the gradients are real.

Write something that matters.
